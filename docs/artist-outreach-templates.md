# Hearo Catalog Artist Outreach Templates

Two templates for inviting artists into the catalog. Template A is for artists whose work would be hosted directly (host tier; demoscene tracker artists with freely-distributable work). Template B is for artists whose commercial work would be linked from Bandcamp (bandcamp tier; living artists with active commercial releases).

Personalize per artist. Replace bracketed placeholders with real values.

## Template A: For artists whose work would be `host` tier

> Subject: Hearo Catalog: would you like a hand in how your work is listed?
>
> Hi [artist],
>
> I'm Tony Atkins, the developer behind Hearo, a DOS audio player for vintage PC hardware. I'm building Hearo Catalog, a small curated index of vintage computer music that the player will browse and stream from on machines from 8088 through Pentium II.
>
> I'd like to include [specific work or works] in the catalog. Your work would be hosted under a published content policy that documents how we have the right to list it, credits you by handle, real name (only if you've published it yourself), group affiliation, year, and original release context, and honors removal requests promptly via either an informal email or formal DMCA notice. The full policy is at https://github.com/tonyuatkins-afk/hearo-catalog/blob/main/COPYRIGHT.md; the takedown procedure is at https://github.com/tonyuatkins-afk/hearo-catalog/blob/main/TAKEDOWN.md.
>
> The reason I'm writing rather than just including the track on the standard demoscene-archive footing: I'd rather you have a hand in how your work appears than discover after the fact. If you'd prefer not to be in the catalog, please tell me and I'll respect that. If you're happy to be in, even better, and if you'd like to suggest other works of yours that should be in the showcase, I'm all ears.
>
> The catalog is non-commercial, MIT-licensed where it isn't yours, and operated by a registered DMCA agent (Hearo Catalog, DMCA-1072235). It's part of a broader retrocomputing project I run alongside Barely Booting on YouTube.
>
> Tony Atkins
> tonyuatkins@gmail.com
> Hearo Catalog: https://hearo.barelybooting.com
> GitHub: https://github.com/tonyuatkins-afk/hearo-catalog

## Template B: For artists whose commercial work would be `bandcamp` tier

> Subject: Hearo Catalog: would you like your work listed with a direct link to your storefront?
>
> Hi [artist],
>
> I'm Tony Atkins, the developer behind Hearo, a DOS audio player for vintage PC hardware. I'm building Hearo Catalog, a small curated index of vintage computer music that the player will browse from on machines from 8088 through Pentium II.
>
> For living artists with commercial releases, the catalog never hosts audio. Instead, we list the work with full metadata (title, year, format, hardware affinity, a short editorial note on why it belongs in the catalog) and a direct link to your Bandcamp or other storefront. The intent is to point listeners at you, not around you.
>
> I'd like permission to list [specific releases] this way. Catalog inclusion plus linkout, no hosting. We credit you by your published name, document the permission in the manifest, and remove on request at any time.
>
> The full content policy is at https://github.com/tonyuatkins-afk/hearo-catalog/blob/main/COPYRIGHT.md; the takedown procedure is at https://github.com/tonyuatkins-afk/hearo-catalog/blob/main/TAKEDOWN.md. The catalog operator is a registered DMCA agent (Hearo Catalog, DMCA-1072235).
>
> If you're interested, a single line in reply ("yes, list X and Y with a link to bandcamp.com/...") is all I need; I'll record the permission in the manifest and send you a link to the entry once it's live.
>
> Tony Atkins
> tonyuatkins@gmail.com
> Hearo Catalog: https://hearo.barelybooting.com
> GitHub: https://github.com/tonyuatkins-afk/hearo-catalog
